How we build these numbers

Where the data comes from, what we count, and how we make sure it is right.

2 · CleanKeep real home sales, drop errors
3 · LinkMatch each deal to a Kotook area, developer and project
4 · CalculateMedians, trends and checks before publishing

1. Where the data comes from

Every number on this site comes from the Dubai Land Department (DLD), the government office that registers every property sale in Dubai, and from Ejari, the official rent-contract register. We download their open-data files (DLD's open-data page and Dubai Pulse), so we use the same records the government publishes. Each update adds only the new rows and then recalculates everything. We do not use asking prices from listing portals.

2. What we count

  • Sales count and total value include every registered sale (homes, land and buildings), the same way DLD reports the market.
  • Prices use only residential apartments and villas. Offices, shops, hotel rooms and land are left out, and so are obvious typing errors (a price per square foot far too low or too high to be real).
  • Off-plan means bought from the developer before the building is finished. Ready means the building is finished; this includes both resales and the developer's sales at handover.
  • Rent uses only new rent contracts for a single home. Renewals are left out because the law caps how much they can rise, so they sit below the real market rent.

3. Linking deals to Kotook

DLD uses its own names for areas and projects, often in Arabic and often different from the names buyers know (for example, Dubai Marina is "Marsa Dubai" in DLD). We built a matching table so each deal lands on the right Kotook area, developer and project.

  • A project is linked only when the developer and the name clearly agree. We do not guess from similar spellings, and unclear cases are left unlinked rather than linked wrongly.
  • Large DLD zones are split by their master community (Motor City, for example, is not lumped into its wider DLD zone).
  • About 300 Kotook projects, 87 areas and 120 developers have enough sales to show numbers today.

4. What each number means

Price per sqftThe median price per square foot over the last 12 months: half of the sales were cheaper and half more expensive. A median is not pulled up by a few luxury sales the way an average is.
Median priceThe middle sale price of the last 12 months.
Yearly changeHow much prices moved compared with the 12 months before, comparing like with like: the same project, the same number of bedrooms and a similar size. This way, a month full of big villas does not look like a price jump.
Off-plan shareThe share of sales bought before completion.
RentThe median yearly rent of new contracts.
Under constructionUnits in projects that DLD lists as not finished yet.

5. Rules that keep the numbers honest

  • Minimum sales. We show a price only when it rests on at least 10 sales, and a yearly change only when both years have at least 20 comparable sales. Otherwise you see "–" instead of a misleading number.
  • Automatic check before publishing. After each update, the system checks that city-wide numbers stay in a realistic range. If anything looks wrong, the new numbers are not published and the previous ones stay live.
  • Monthly charts move up and down because each month sells a different mix of homes. Look at the 12-month figures for the real level.

6. How we checked it

  • Market totals: our 2025 total (214,826 sales, AED 682 billion) is within 1% of DLD's own published total (215,736 sales, AED 687 billion).
  • Two sources, same answer: where the DLD website files and Dubai Pulse overlap, they give almost the same numbers (5,856 vs 5,828 sales; 1,437 vs 1,438 AED per sqft).
  • Independent report: we compared Business Bay rents for 2025 with a published Property Monitor report: 17,891 vs 17,134 contracts and 118.7 vs 119 AED per sqft, with almost the same rent for each bedroom count.
  • Matching: we checked a sample of project links by hand; about 1 in 40 was wrong and was fixed.

7. Limits to keep in mind

  • Sales are updated to 14 November 2026. Rent data currently ends in February 2026, so rental yield is not shown until newer rent files arrive.
  • About 3 in 10 of last year's sales can be linked to a specific Kotook project; the rest still count for their area and the whole market.
  • Ready sales include developer handover sales, because DLD data cannot tell them apart from resales.

In short: official government records, real homes only, strict matching to Kotook, medians instead of averages, and no number shown when there is too little data behind it.
